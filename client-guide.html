Finding Clients

Core principle: Every outbound move should feel like a warm introduction. ZDM leads with insight — a competitor analysis, a free audit finding, a specific data point — never a generic pitch.

Outbound Channels

LinkedIn Prospecting

Target founders, CMOs, and marketing directors at companies matching the ICP. Use Sales Navigator to filter by company size, revenue signals, and recent job changes. Send a 3-touch sequence: connection + personalized note → value DM (share relevant stat or audit finding) → soft CTA to a 20-min call.

Volume: 15 new connections/day, 5 follow-ups/day.

Cold Email Outreach

Use Apollo.io or Hunter.io to source verified emails. Personalize line 1 with a specific company observation (recent product launch, Glassdoor growth signal, ad spend estimate from SimilarWeb). Keep under 90 words. CTA = 1 question, not a meeting request.

Volume: 30–50 sends/day from warmed domain. Target 40%+ open rate.

Referral System

Every client gets a referral ask at 90 days (when results are first visible). Offer a one-month retainer credit per signed referral. Send a templated "thinking of you" email to past clients quarterly with a new insight or case study to stay top of mind.

Target: 30% of new clients from referral within 12 months.

Inbound Content

Publish one case study per month (anonymized if needed) with hard numbers — ROAS improvement, CPL reduction, revenue impact. Share on LinkedIn and embed in a ZDM newsletter. Each case study should include a specific industry keyword for organic search.

Distribution: LinkedIn post → email list → website blog.

Paid Prospecting (Meta)

Run a low-budget awareness campaign ($500–$800/mo) retargeting LinkedIn profile visitors and website visitors. Creative = short-form video with a specific stat ("We reduced this brand's CPL by 47%"). CTA to a free audit landing page.

Budget: Reinvest 10% of retainer revenue into ZDM's own ads.

Strategic Partnerships

Build referral relationships with web development agencies, Shopify developers, brand designers, and business coaches. They have clients who need paid media — we send them overflow creative projects. Set up a mutual referral agreement in writing.

Target: 5 active referral partners maintained at all times.

03

Qualifying & Closing

Discovery Call Framework (BANT+)

  1. Budget

    Ask: "What's your current monthly marketing spend, and what portion is allocated to paid channels?" Minimum viable: $5K/mo retainer + $15K/mo ad spend. If below threshold, offer a scaled entry package or refer out.

  2. Authority

    Confirm you're speaking with a decision-maker. Ask: "Who else is involved in choosing a marketing partner?" If it's a gatekeeper, request a follow-up call with the founder or CMO before investing more time.

  3. Need

    Uncover the real pain. Ask: "What's the #1 metric you'd move to consider this a success in 90 days?" Listen for specific numbers — CAC, ROAS, leads/month. Vague answers = not sales-ready yet.

  4. Timeline

    Ask: "When are you looking to get started?" If the answer is "eventually" or "maybe Q3," set a calendar reminder and move on. A deal with no urgency is not a deal.

  5. Tech & Data Access

    Confirm: Do they have a pixel installed? GA4 set up with conversions tracked? CRM in use? Access to ad account history? No tracking infrastructure = 30-day setup phase before campaigns can run.

  6. Competitive Context

    Ask: "Are you currently working with any other agencies, or evaluating anyone else?" Understand what alternatives they're considering. This tells you how to position the proposal and what objections to pre-empt.

Proposal & Close Process

Proposal Delivery

  • Send within 48 hours of discovery call
  • Lead with their specific problem and the revenue at stake
  • Show a 90-day roadmap with milestones (not a list of services)
  • Include one comparable case study with hard numbers
  • Present 2 package options — never 3 or more
  • Use the ZDM Proposal Tool for delivery

Objection Handling

  • "It's too expensive" — Reframe to cost per acquisition. Ask: "If we get you 50 leads at $X, what does that close rate mean for revenue?"
  • "We need to think about it" — Set a 48-hour follow-up call. Ask what specific concerns need resolving.
  • "We tried ads before and they didn't work" — Ask for the account. Do a live audit on the call to show specifically what went wrong.
  • "Can you guarantee results?" — Never guarantee. Commit to a 60-day performance review clause in the contract.

Contract Essentials

  • 3-month minimum engagement — no month-to-month until month 4
  • Ad spend billed separately from management fee (client owns the ad accounts)
  • Net-7 payment terms on retainer invoice; Net-0 on ad spend deposits
  • 30-day written notice to cancel after initial term
  • IP clause: all creative assets, landing pages, and copy belong to client on exit
  • 60-day performance review: if KPIs miss by more than 30%, renegotiate or exit cleanly
  • Non-compete: ZDM will not onboard a direct competitor in the same geo for 6 months

Problems ZDM Solves

E-Commerce

High CPA With No Clear Cause

Ad spend is rising but cost per acquisition keeps climbing. Usually caused by creative fatigue, audience saturation, or a tracking gap that's hiding true performance. ZDM runs a full-funnel attribution audit, refreshes creative systematically, and restructures campaign architecture to isolate what's actually driving cost.

Both

Tracking Is Broken or Incomplete

Platforms are reporting conversions but the numbers don't match Shopify or the CRM. This happens post-iOS 14 without server-side events, or when GTM tags fire incorrectly. ZDM implements Conversions API, audits the data layer, and reconciles attribution across GA4, Meta, and Google so reporting is trustworthy.

B2B

Leads Don't Convert to Revenue

The form fills are coming in but the pipeline doesn't move. Usually a qualification problem — campaigns are optimizing for volume over quality. ZDM tightens audience targeting, adds qualifying friction to forms, and implements offline conversion tracking so the algorithm learns from closed-won deals, not just submissions.

E-Commerce

ROAS Collapses After Scaling

Performance is great at $5K/mo spend but falls apart at $20K. Classic audience exhaustion and bid competition. ZDM uses a modular scaling framework: expand to new audience layers, introduce new creative angles, and distribute budget across geographic or demographic segments before the primary audience saturates.

Both

No Retargeting Strategy

All budget is going to cold acquisition with nothing recapturing the 95% of visitors who don't convert. ZDM builds a full retargeting stack: 1-day, 7-day, and 30-day windows with distinct creative and offers for each. For e-commerce: cart abandonment sequences. For B2B: case study and testimonial retargeting.

B2B

LinkedIn Ads Burning Budget With No Results

LinkedIn CPCs are high by design — but most B2B brands misuse it by running brand awareness to cold audiences with a "contact us" CTA. ZDM restructures LinkedIn campaigns around gated content lead magnets, retargets video viewers and website visitors with case studies, and uses Matched Audiences to target exact company lists.

E-Commerce

Creative Is Stale and Performing Poorly

The same three ads have been running for six months. Creative is the biggest performance lever in paid media — more impactful than targeting or bidding. ZDM installs a creative testing machine: 8–12 new ad variants per month, structured A/B tests on hooks, formats and CTAs, and a systematic kill/scale framework based on 72-hour data.

Both

No Visibility Into What's Actually Working

Marketing reports show clicks and impressions but nothing tied to revenue. Leadership can't make decisions. ZDM builds a Looker Studio dashboard that connects ad platforms to GA4 and the CRM, surfaces CAC by channel, ROAS by campaign, and pipeline velocity — giving the team a single source of truth updated daily.

E-Commerce

Google Shopping Losing to Competitors

Products are appearing in Shopping but at low impression share, losing to competitors on price or relevance. ZDM audits the Merchant Center feed for title and description optimization, restructures Shopping campaigns by margin tier, and implements Performance Max with proper asset group segmentation and audience signals.

Both

Agency Relationship Feels Like a Black Box

Previous agency sent a PDF once a month with numbers but no context, strategy, or proactive communication. ZDM operates with full transparency: clients have live access to the reporting dashboard at all times, a weekly Loom performance update every Monday, and a 60-minute monthly strategy call where we show our work and explain every decision.

Creative is the primary variable in paid media performance. ZDM treats creative like a media channel — systematic testing, not gut-feel design. Every piece has a hypothesis behind it.

Monthly Creative Cadence

  1. Creative Review (Day 1)

    Pull performance data on all active ads. Identify the top 3 performers and bottom 3. The top performers get iterated; the bottom 3 get killed or paused.

  2. Brief Writing (Day 2–3)

    Write creative briefs for 8–12 new variants. Each brief specifies: the audience pain point being addressed, the hook format (text overlay, spoken, or visual), the proof element (stat, testimonial, demo), and the CTA.

  3. Production (Day 4–10)

    Static ads produced in-house using Figma + brand kit. Video ads: use client UGC, stock footage, or brief a content creator. All videos under 30 seconds with a hook in the first 2 seconds.

  4. Review & Approval (Day 11–12)

    Internal review against brief. Client approval for any new messaging angles. No approval required for creative iterations of already-approved angles.

  5. Launch & Monitor (Day 13–15)

    Upload to ad platform. Monitor CTR, CPC, and hook rate (% watched past 3 seconds) at 72 hours. A/B test one variable at a time.

Creative Formats & When to Use Them

Static Image

  • Best for: product shots, before/after, offers, testimonial quotes
  • Ideal ratio: 1:1 (feed) and 9:16 (stories)
  • Hook: bold headline text overlay, max 7 words
  • Use for: Google Display, Meta feed, LinkedIn Sponsored

Short-Form Video (6–30s)

  • Best for: product demos, UGC testimonials, problem/solution
  • Ideal ratio: 9:16 for Reels/TikTok; 1:1 for feed
  • Hook: pattern interrupt in first 1.5 seconds
  • Use for: Meta Reels, TikTok Ads, YouTube Shorts

Carousel

  • Best for: product collections, step-by-step processes, feature lists
  • Card 1 = hook, Cards 2–4 = proof, Card 5 = CTA
  • Consistent visual style across all cards
  • Use for: Meta feed retargeting, LinkedIn document ads

Long-Form Video (60s–3min)

  • Best for: brand story, detailed product explanations, case studies
  • Requires strong scripting — use problem → agitation → solution structure
  • Repurpose to YouTube pre-roll and Meta retargeting
  • Use for: warm and hot audiences only — too expensive for cold

Monthly SEO Deliverables

  • 2–4 long-form blog posts targeting bottom-funnel keywords
  • Technical audit check: Core Web Vitals, crawl errors, broken links
  • 1 link-building outreach campaign (HARO, guest post, or digital PR)
  • Monthly rank tracking report for target 30 keywords
  • Schema markup implementation for new content
  • Internal linking pass on new and existing content

Keyword Strategy

  • Bottom-funnel first: "[product] buy online," "[service] near me," "[brand] review" — these convert
  • Mid-funnel: "best [category]," "[problem] solution" — captures research phase
  • Top-funnel: Educational content that seeds retargeting audiences
  • Target keywords with search volume 500–5,000/mo — less competition, real traffic
  • Cluster keywords by topic, not individually

Content → Paid Media Flywheel

Every SEO article is a retargeting audience seed. Readers of a "best [product type]" article are categorized as research-phase prospects — they get retargeted with a comparison ad and a discount offer. High-performing ad copy is reverse-engineered into organic headlines and meta descriptions. Paid data informs SEO strategy: if "free shipping" crushes in ads, it becomes the hero headline on the landing page and the title tag.

Email and SMS are the highest-ROI owned channels. ZDM builds these as revenue recovery systems — not newsletters. Every flow exists to recover lost revenue or extend customer LTV.

Core Email Flows (E-Commerce)

Welcome Series (5 emails)

  • Email 1 (0hr): Welcome + brand story + 10% offer
  • Email 2 (24hr): Social proof + bestseller showcase
  • Email 3 (48hr): Objection handling (returns, quality, origin)
  • Email 4 (72hr): User-generated content + community
  • Email 5 (5 days): Last chance offer + urgency

Abandoned Cart (3 emails)

  • Email 1 (1hr): Soft reminder — "still thinking it over?"
  • Email 2 (24hr): Reinforce value + address top objections
  • Email 3 (72hr): Discount offer or free shipping threshold
  • Target recovery rate: 8–12% of abandoned carts

Post-Purchase (4 emails)

  • Email 1 (0hr): Confirmation + order summary
  • Email 2 (3 days): Product education / how to use
  • Email 3 (7 days): Review request
  • Email 4 (21 days): Cross-sell / complementary product

Win-Back (3 emails)

  • Trigger: no purchase in 90 days
  • Email 1: "We miss you" + new arrivals
  • Email 2: Personalized recommendation based on past purchase
  • Email 3: Best offer + sunset warning (last email)

Communication & Reporting Cadence

  1. Weekly Loom — Every Monday by 10am

    3–5 minute screen recording walking through the previous week's performance. Cover: spend vs. budget, top performers, any anomalies, and what's changing this week. No slide deck needed — Loom on the live dashboard.

  2. Monthly Strategy Call — First Thursday of the Month

    60-minute video call. Agenda: review the month's data, revisit 90-day goals, present the next month's test roadmap, surface any budget reallocation recommendations, and address client questions.

  3. Monthly Written Report — By the 5th of Each Month

    Sent via email. Includes: executive summary (3 sentences max), key metrics vs. prior month vs. targets, creative performance breakdown, SEO ranking changes if applicable, and recommended actions.

  4. Quarterly Business Review (QBR) — Every 3 Months

    90-minute call with founder/CMO. Review the full quarter: what worked, what didn't, competitive landscape changes, LTV and CAC trends, and revised targets for the next quarter. Prepare a deck for this one.

  5. Real-Time Dashboard — Always On

    Client has live access to the Looker Studio dashboard at all times. No need to request data — if they have a question at 11pm, they can look themselves. Reduces urgent Slack messages and builds trust.

Client Objection Scripts

These are the 12 most common objections you'll hear on a discovery or closing call. For each one: understand the real fear underneath, then respond to the fear — not the words. Don't memorise scripts — understand the logic, then speak naturally.

OBJ 01 "Your prices are too high."
+

Real fear: They've been burned before and don't believe the ROI will materialise. Or they're comparing retainer cost to ad spend and confusing the two numbers.

Response:

"I hear you — and I'd push back on one thing. The question isn't whether $3,500 is a lot to spend. The question is: what does it cost you to keep running ads without a proper system behind them? If your current ROAS is 1.8 and we get you to 3.5, that's not a cost — that's leverage. What's your current cost per acquisition?"

Follow-up: Anchor to their numbers, not yours. If they can't answer the question, that's your proof they need the service.

OBJ 02 "We tried ads before and it didn't work."
+

Real fear: They wasted money and don't trust the channel — or the people running it.

"That tells me more about who ran them than whether ads work for your business. Can you walk me through what you ran — what platform, what the offer was, and what the tracking looked like? In 90% of cases, the issue is either the offer, the audience, or broken attribution — not the platform itself. Let me show you what an audit of your previous account would find."

Follow-up: Offer a free audit of their previous ad account. This turns the objection into a sales conversation — you'll find problems, and problems are your pitch.

OBJ 03 "I need to think about it."
+

Real fear: Either a specific concern they haven't voiced, or they need to check with someone else. "I need to think" almost always means "I have a question I haven't asked yet."

"Of course — I'd expect nothing less. I want you to be sure. Can I ask: is it the investment, the timing, or is there something specific you're not yet certain about? Because if there's a real question underneath, I'd rather answer it now than leave you with it."

Follow-up: Surface the real objection. Then schedule a concrete next-step call — never leave it as "I'll follow up in a few days."

OBJ 04 "We're already working with another agency."
+

Real fear: Switching cost and disruption anxiety. But if they're taking your call, something isn't working.

"That's good to know — and I'm curious: what made you take this call today? Most people don't explore options when everything's running perfectly. I'm not asking you to switch anything right now. But I'd like to run a complimentary audit of your current account — takes us 48 hours, costs you nothing, and either confirms your agency is doing a great job or shows you exactly where revenue is being left on the table."

Follow-up: The free audit is your wedge. Don't pitch a switch — pitch a second opinion.

OBJ 05 "We don't have the budget right now."
+

Real fear: Cash flow concern, or the investment genuinely doesn't feel justified yet.

"Understood. Let me ask you this — if we could show you that our Starter package pays for itself within 60 days based on your current ad spend and conversion rate, would budget still be the issue? I'm not asking you to take that on faith. Let's look at the numbers together. What are you currently spending on ads per month?"

Follow-up: If genuine budget issue, offer Starter at $1,500. If no ad budget at all, they're not your client yet — refer them to the v2 $200 boost plan as a starting point.

OBJ 06 "Can you guarantee results?"
+

Real fear: They've been overpromised before. They want certainty — which is understandable. Honesty here builds more trust than a fake guarantee.

"No ethical marketing agency can guarantee specific revenue numbers — anyone who says otherwise is lying to you. What I can guarantee is the process: a data-driven system, full transparency on what's working, weekly optimisation, and we don't hide from bad months. We report everything. What we've found is that clients who commit to the 90-day system see measurable improvement in ROAS 100% of the time — not because of luck, but because the process removes the guesswork."

Follow-up: Offer a money-back guarantee on the first month if they don't see a meaningful improvement in tracking and baseline metrics.

OBJ 07 "We can do it in-house."
+

Real fear: They want to retain control, or believe an in-house hire is cheaper.

"Totally valid approach. A mid-level performance marketer costs $60–85K salary plus tools, plus time to onboard and ramp. You get one person covering one channel, trained on your account. With ZDM you get a team across paid media, analytics, and CRO — active from day one, with benchmarks from dozens of accounts. If you're at the scale where an in-house team makes sense, we'd genuinely recommend it. But most businesses aren't there yet."

Follow-up: Ask about their timeline to hire and what the ramp-up period looks like. Usually 3–6 months — that's 3–6 months of ad spend without optimisation.

OBJ 08 "You don't have experience in our industry."
+

Real fear: Concern that there'll be a learning curve they'll pay for.

"That's actually often an advantage. An agency that's only ever worked in your sector tends to bring the same ideas. We bring cross-industry frameworks — what we've seen work in e-commerce, we apply to B2B. What works in SaaS acquisition, we test in retail. The platforms and the psychology don't change — only the creative and the offer do. And you know your industry better than anyone. We supply the system, you supply the domain knowledge."

Follow-up: Mention a transferable case study from an adjacent sector. Focus on the metrics, not the industry.

OBJ 09 "We're too small / not ready for this yet."
+

Real fear: They don't believe their business can absorb or convert the traffic. Sometimes this is true — qualify carefully.

"What does 'ready' look like to you? Because in my experience, the businesses that wait until they're 'ready' usually wait until they're losing to someone who wasn't. If you have a product that sells, a website that converts at even 1%, and $500/month for ads — you have enough to start building a system. We start lean and scale what works."

Follow-up: If they genuinely have no ad budget or no offer clarity, they're not a fit today. Nurture them for 60–90 days and check back in. Don't force a bad fit.

OBJ 10 "I need to speak to my business partner / board first."
+

Real fear: Legitimate stakeholder process. Don't fight it — equip them to sell it internally.

"Completely understand. Can I make that conversation easier for you? I'll send you a one-page summary of exactly what we're proposing, the projected outcomes based on your numbers, and what you're currently leaving on the table. That way you're not selling them cold — you have the data. When would you be speaking with them?"

Follow-up: Send the one-pager within 2 hours. Set a hard follow-up call 48hrs after their internal meeting.

OBJ 11 "The last agency we used disappeared after onboarding."
+

Real fear: They paid for access and then got ignored. The agency relationship felt like a black box.

"That's unfortunately common — and it's a valid fear. Here's how we're different: you get a live Looker Studio dashboard that updates daily, weekly reports regardless of how the numbers look, and a named account lead who picks up the phone. If you ever feel like you don't know what's happening with your money, something has gone wrong on our end. We've built our process around that specifically."

Follow-up: Offer to introduce them to a current client they can speak to directly. Social proof from a real person neutralises this fear better than any script.

OBJ 12 "What makes you different from every other agency?"
+

Real fear: They've heard every pitch before. They want evidence, not positioning.

"Honestly? I'm not going to tell you we're different — I'm going to show you. Give me your ad account access for 48 hours. We'll do a complimentary audit and come back with three specific things your current setup is getting wrong and what we'd change. If what we find isn't compelling, you owe us nothing and we part on good terms. If it is — we have something to talk about."

Follow-up: The free audit is your answer to every version of this question. Action beats claims every time.
